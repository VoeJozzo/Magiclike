Bleach strips a creature of all color (sticker sets color to colorless) and then exiles it. This frame enacts both halves of the mechanic at once: the creature is rendered as a featureless chalk-white void — every trace of color drained out of it — and it is visibly fading from existence, fraying into rising white sparks at its feet and tail (the exile). Crucially, it sits against a vivid crimson-and-orange autumn forest in full color, so the white shape reads as a hole punched in a colored world rather than just an albino animal — the contrast is what makes "color was removed" legible at 64x32. The silhouette is a clean, dynamic mid-stride wolf, the single most readable shape in the batch. It beats gen 5 (shape read more horse than wolf, less dissolution) and gen 9 (better mote-trail but muted, washed-out background that softened the color contrast). Gen 7 is the only frame that lands all three at once: colorless, vanishing, and a strong silhouette with the punchiest color-contrast background.
