Squire of Oaths is a literal/character card (vanilla 2/3 white Human Soldier, no keywords or effects), so the art's job is to depict the character honestly and let the name carry the meaning. Gen 4 enacts "of Oaths" directly: a young squire in plate and a white-and-gold tabard kneels on one knee in clean side profile, both hands on the pommel of a tall vertical longsword grounded before him, head bowed mid-vow, flanked by lit candle-stands and crimson banners in a stone chapel. The kneeling-figure-plus-vertical-sword silhouette is crisp and instantly legible at 64x32 and reads unmistakably as swearing fealty. Color identity is strongly white (white tabard, gold trim, warm candlelight) and the face and pose are the cleanest of the five rolls. It beat the warm-light tweak (gen 5), whose glowing blade drifted toward "magical sword" and away from a solemn oath, and the earlier kneeling rolls (gens 2-3) whose faces were muddier and framing more cramped.
