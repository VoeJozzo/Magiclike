This frame best enacts Worldly Tutor's mechanic: the card searches your library for a creature and pulls it to hand, so the art shows a green-robed, grey-haired druid kneeling at the forest's edge, beckoning with a blazing sphere of green summoning-light in his open palm, while a great antlered stag steps out of the dark treeline to answer the call. It is unmistakably "calling a creature out of the world to your side," not a generic spellcaster. Compared to the other rolls it wins on every axis that matters: the cleanest two-figure composition with both silhouettes fully readable at 64x32 (the seed-locked tweak preserved gen 1's clear staging), the brightest and most legible mechanic-enacting glow (the green light in the palm with motes rising), the crispest stag rendering with detailed antlers, strong green color identity, and an arching-trunk forest vignette that frames the scene with real depth. Gen 6 is a strong, more intimate runner-up but its robe reads dark and its pose is more ambiguous; the orb-of-vision direction (gen 2) never rendered a creature inside the orb, so it read as a generic green caster. Gen 5 is the clear keeper.
