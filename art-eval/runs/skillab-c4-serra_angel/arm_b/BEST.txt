This roll is the cleanest mechanic-and-icon read of the eight. Serra Angel is a vanilla-shaped character card (4/4 White Angel, Vigilance, no triggers), so the art's job is to nail the archetype and enact the keyword as a character trait: Vigilance = the tireless sentinel who strikes while still on guard. Gen 5 shows a frontal, symmetric warrior angel in white-and-gold plate, broad feathered wings spread to fill the width, a single legible silver longsword carried forward across the body, and a soft glowing golden halo above the head — armed and mid-descent yet alert and watchful, which is exactly the "attacks without lowering her guard" beat. It has the strongest silhouette test of the set (cruciform body + spread wings, instantly readable at 64x32), disciplined color identity (white feathers and gold-edged dawn clouds, no palette drift), and a named light source carrying the scene. It harmonizes with the set's existing angel arts (Benevolent Angel's frontal-wings-and-halo language) while deliberately contrasting them by being martial and poised to strike rather than serene. It came from a seed-locked tweak of Gen 2 that improved the halo from a hard rectangle to a glowing ring and sharpened the single-blade read. Gen 3 (best motion) and Gen 7 (its own refinement) were close runners-up; Gen 5 edges them on crispest wing edges plus the most legible single sword.
