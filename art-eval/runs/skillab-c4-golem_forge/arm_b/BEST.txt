Golem Forge turns a Land into a 4/4 Artifact Creature, so the art has to enact a transformation: raw land being forged into a living construct. Gen 4 is the cleanest, most commanding read of that beat. A massive dark-iron and brass golem heaves itself upward out of a field of molten rubble and glowing embers, with cracked earth and forge-heat spread across the foreground ground so the land genuinely looks like it is being forged into the rising body. The silhouette is unmistakably a hulking humanoid construct at 64x32, the palette is true colorless-artifact (riveted iron, soot-stained brass, no chromatic magic), and the only light is the forge-hot core and molten seams, exactly as prompted. Gen 6 was the dramatic runner-up (lava-cavern framing) but its purple cast drifts off the card's pure colorless identity, and gens 1-3/5/7 either read as a finished golem standing rather than emerging, or had softer silhouettes. Gen 4 best unites mechanic-enactment, silhouette legibility, and color discipline.
