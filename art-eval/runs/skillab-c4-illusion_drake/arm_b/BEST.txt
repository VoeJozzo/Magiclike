Gen 01 earns the nomination because it enacts the card's identity most directly and reads cleanly at 64x32. The Illusion Drake is a vanilla creature whose entire conceptual weight is carried by its type — "Illusion" — and the image answers the mechanic-in-art question by rendering the dragon as visibly translucent, glassy, and phantom: its icy cerulean body shows the deep blue sky through it via a natural checkerboard dithering effect on the wings, and the whole form has a blue-white crystal luminescence that says "this is not a real dragon." The creature is unmistakably airborne in clear left-facing flight profile (mandatory for a creature with flying), filling the frame with a single large clean silhouette. The palette is committed to blue identity — cerulean, ice-white, sapphire — matching the card's 1UU cost. Compared to the conjurer scenes (gens 06-07), which also enact the mechanic well with more narrative richness, gen 01 gives the drake itself the full frame rather than splitting attention, which reads better as card art for a creature card where the creature should dominate. Compared to gens 04-05 (head-on V-wing), gen 01 is more readable as a dragon in flight than as a bat-shaped silhouette. It is the clearest, most immediate answer to "what does an Illusion Drake look like?"
