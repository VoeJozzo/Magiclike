This frame best enacts the card's mechanic. Faithless Looting draws two cards and discards two; the art shows a crimson-cloaked looter hunched over a roaring fire-basin, hands reaching into the flames as he feeds scrolls to the fire while scattered parchments litter the floor around a hoard — the grab-and-burn churn of "draw two, throw two away" made literal. It carries strong red color identity through the firelight (the only light source), has a clean readable silhouette at 64x32 (figure on the right, bright fire-basin on the left, scattered scrolls along the bottom), and reads as a captured moment of greedy, desperate rummaging rather than a static portrait. Of the four rolls it has the clearest, best-defined figure-and-action and the most dramatic firelight; gen 4 was atmospheric but its figure read darker and less legible, and gens 1/2 implied the discard gesture less crisply. This is a seed-locked tweak of gen 2 that sharpened exactly the looting-at-the-fire action.
