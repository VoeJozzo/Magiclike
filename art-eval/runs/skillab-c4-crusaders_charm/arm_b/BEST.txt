This frame enacts the card's defining mechanic: Crusader's Charm is a three-mode white spell (Smite / Embolden / Sanctuary), and the art maps each mode to a visibly distinct colored beam fanning out from a holy talisman held aloft by a kneeling golden-armored crusader — a red beam upper-left (Smite, deal 2), a gold-white beam straight up (Embolden, +2/+2), and a blue beam upper-right (Sanctuary, gain life and draw). This mirrors the Verdant-Charm modal-art pattern the skill holds up as the exemplar: three effects, three rays, three colors, all cleanly separated rather than collapsing into a single white wash. The dim slate-gray night background (chosen specifically to keep the colored beams from being eaten by a sunset) lets all three modes read clearly. White/gold color identity is intact, the central kneeling-crusader silhouette is strong and legible at 64x32, and the fanned composition is dynamic. It beats gen 5 (which fused the three colors into one parallel vertical stripe, reading as one beam not three) and gens 1-2 (single dramatic beam, modality lost).
