This frame enacts Curse Shade's mechanic — "when this creature dies, exile a creature" — more legibly than any other roll. A hooded specter of dark smoke looms from the upper-right with its lower body visibly fraying into wisps (the shade is itself dying), reaching one long arm down to a pale steel-armored soldier sprawled on his back in the lower-left, screaming, his body dissolving into pale-green ash as he is unmade. A bright pale-green spectral plume — the exile glow — rises between them as the only light source. The dual unmaking (the shade dies AND drags its victim out of existence) reads in a single glance, which is the whole point of the card. The pale armor contrasts hard against the dark shade so the victim reads as a distinct subject rather than a second ghost (the color-trap fix that gens 4 and 6 failed). Color identity is clean black-plus-spectral-green, and the composition harmonizes with the set's existing shade/wraith arts (dread_wraith, consume_spirit) that put a dying victim in the lower foreground beneath a looming dark figure. Gen 8 was the most atmospheric runner-up but its looming figure read as a solid grounded ghoul rather than a fraying, dying specter, softening the mechanic; Gen 7 keeps the rule front and center. The only visible flaw is a small red speck at the grip point — minor and not worth risking the locked composition to chase.
