Tide Charm is a modal blue spell — Counter / Bounce / Foresee — so its art should mirror Verdant Charm's discipline: each mode rendered as a distinct, visible element. Gen 5 does this better than any other roll. A crisp hooded sapphire-robed tidecaller stands arms-spread, face actually readable under the hood (the chief flaw of the earlier rolls is fixed here). The three modes each land as their own beat: a curling cerulean wave on the left (Bounce), a glowing aquamarine scrying orb in his hand plus the bright water-burst above (Foresee), and his blue magic meeting an orange fire on the right (Counter — visibly stopping a spell). The fire is deliberate warm contrast against an otherwise richly blue, color-identity-correct frame, and the silhouette reads instantly at 64x32 as "water-mage casting three things at once." It is the cleanest subject AND the strongest mechanic-in-art of the set; gen 6's symmetric water-halo was prettier as pure atmosphere but collapsed the three modes into one aura, and gens 1/3 had darker, less legible faces.
