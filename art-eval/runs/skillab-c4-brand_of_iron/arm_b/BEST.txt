This roll best enacts the card's mechanic: Brand of Iron is a cheap red sorcery that permanently makes a creature an Artifact, so the art has to show a living thing being turned to metal by a brand. Gen 4 is a whole snarling beast in clean side profile whose body is visibly mid-transformation — fiery orange-glowing seams of iron creep along its flank and hindquarters from the searing brand point, the living gray fur giving way to molten metal. The transformation IS the image, not a decorative aside. Red color identity saturates the frame (red forge backdrop, orange brand-fire as the sole light source), and at 64x32 the silhouette reads instantly as a single branded beast. Compared to the alternatives it wins on mechanic legibility: gen 5 turned the creature fully to metal and lost the act of transformation, gen 6 reads more like a boar wearing armor with the fire detached to the side, and gens 1-3 lost the creature or the brand. Gen 4 keeps the flesh, the metal, and the searing all in one readable scene.
