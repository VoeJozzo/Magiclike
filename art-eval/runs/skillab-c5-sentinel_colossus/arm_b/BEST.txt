Nominated because it is the only frame that ENACTS the card's mechanic rather than merely decorating its theme. Sentinel Colossus's one mechanic is Trample, and trample is an overrun: a force that does not stop, it walks through things. Gen 08 shows exactly that — a heavy verdigris-bronze construct leaning forward mid-stride, emerging from and shattering a dark crumbling stone mass on the left, with broken masonry and dust scattered through the air. The forward lean and airborne debris give it motion and unstoppability that the other rolls lack. The colossus's amber eye glows, the colorless artifact identity is honored in the metal/stone palette (no off-color WUBRG), and it harmonizes materially with the set's construct anchor (alloy_construct) while distinguishing itself through scale and violent motion.

Runner-up was gen 05: a more monumental, cleaner-silhouette giant looming over a tiny soldier — a gorgeous "6/6 Sentinel" portrait. But it is static; it depicts scale and menace, not the trample overrun. Per the mechanic-in-art principle, the frame that reaches for the actual rule (gen 08), despite a slightly murkier lower-body contact, beats the cleaner-but-static portrait that only decorates the construct theme.
