Rationale: This is the only frame where all three beats of the card's death-trigger mechanic co-exist legibly. Curse Shade's defining rule is that when it DIES (battlefield -> graveyard) it EXILES a creature -- a spiteful final act that drags a victim out of existence entirely. Gen 6 enacts exactly that: a looming hooded specter (tattered violet/black rags, hollow face, glowing pale-violet eyes -- consistent with the set's reaper_shade / dread_wraith anchors) stands on the left and reaches a long clawed arm to the right, gripping a distinct, readable armored soldier and pulling him toward a bright violet void orb. Two separate figures both read at 64x32; the void reads unmistakably as exile (erasure, not mere death); a leaning graveyard cross anchors the setting and palette. It tells the whole story -- subject + action + exile-destination -- which the strongest alternatives only do in part (Gen 8's victim-in-portal is crisp but the specter-to-victim grip is weaker; Gen 7/Gen 9 have the cleanest void rings but the victim is faint/absent). Per the skill's "reward the frame that reaches for the whole scene" discipline, Gen 6 reaches and lands.
