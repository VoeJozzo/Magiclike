Flame Summoner is a 2/2 Human Shaman whose only special trait is an enters-the-battlefield trigger: deal 2 damage to any target. So the art has to enact "arrives and immediately strikes," not just depict a shaman. Gen 4 does exactly that, and reads instantly at 64x32: a clearly-lit crimson-robed shaman on the left, mid-cast with his arm thrust forward; a crisp white-hot fireball bursting at the midpoint; and a legible four-legged maned beast on the right, head-down and recoiling with flame licking off it. The three-beat left-to-right composition (caster -> impact -> victim) makes the "deal 2 damage to a creature" mechanic unmistakable, which is the whole point per the mechanic-in-art principle. It sits squarely in the set's red-burn register established by the Char anchor: warm firelight as the sole light source against a deep teal-green old-growth forest, with framing tree arches.

It was chosen over its two close siblings in the same direction. Gen 1 has a more continuous hand-to-victim fire stream but a muddier, less-readable victim. Gen 3 has the most legible beast but a too-dark shaman. Gen 4 is the synthesis: it brightens and clarifies the caster (gen 1's strength) while keeping a readable creature victim and balanced framing (gen 3's strengths), with the clearest moment-of-impact burst of the three. Its one diagnosable flaw -- the far arm/hand blends slightly into the fire -- is minor and is the acceptable cost of a frame that reaches for a real action, which the skill prefers over a timid, generic alternative. (Gen 2, the passive "conjuring a small flame" reading, was ruled out because nothing is being damaged, so it fails the mechanic-in-art gate.)
