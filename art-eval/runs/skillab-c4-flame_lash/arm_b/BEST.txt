Flame Lash deals 4 damage to a creature, and the card's name points straight at the depiction: a *lash* of flame, not a fireball or bolt. This frame nails that. A red-caped pyromancer in side profile at the left cracks a single clean, sinuous arc of orange-crimson flame up and over to a hulking dark beast at the right — the whip reads instantly as one legible silhouette mid-strike, which is exactly the silhouette test at 64x32. It enacts the mechanic on both axes: the lash is the literal flame whip (the name), and the beast is unmistakably the victim taking the hit (the 4 damage to a creature). Color identity is disciplined red-on-ash with the only light coming from the burning lash, and it's a real scene — caster, victim, scorched ground, small fires — not a sprite on void. Gen 6 (the lash literally coiling around the beast) and gen 2 (a straighter horizontal whip) were the runners-up, but gen 5 has the cleanest gestalt: at a glance it reads as a mage whipping fire across a monster, with the best balance of a readable lash arc and clear heavy-hit stakes.
