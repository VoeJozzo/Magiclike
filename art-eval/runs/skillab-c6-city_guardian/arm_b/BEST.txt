This frame is the strongest enactment of City Guardian's mechanic: a legendary white-and-gold-armored soldier planted dead-center in the open stone arch of a fortified city gate — the literal chokepoint that every traveler (every card) must pass through and pay a toll at, which is exactly what "All cards cost {1} more to cast" does to the game. The composition reads instantly at 64x32: a crisp armored silhouette framed by the gate arch, red banners flanking, a tall gold-headed halberd, the city and sky glowing through the passage behind him. Color identity is correct for a White card (white/silver plate with gold trim, red cloak for contrast and motion), and the ornate gold detailing plus the lone heroic stance sell the Legendary, First-Strike-ready Human Soldier. The one unlanded beat across the set was the halberd held horizontally to physically bar the gate (the model's soldier prior strongly favored an upright polearm), but the gate-guardian-as-tollkeeper story is unmistakable here, and the frame's overall gestalt is the cleanest and most dynamic of the eight rolls. Runner-up was gen_04 (same gate-arch concept, slightly flatter/more frontal).
