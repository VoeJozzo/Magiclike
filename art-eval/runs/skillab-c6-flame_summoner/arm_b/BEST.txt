This frame best enacts the card's mechanic rather than merely decorating its theme. Flame Summoner's only rule is an enters-the-battlefield trigger that deals 2 damage to a creature or player, so the art should show that damage being dealt to a target the moment the shaman arrives. Gen 5 does exactly that: a crimson-caped human shaman in a wide side-profile stance thrusts his hand forward and a burst of orange fire fires from his palm into a charging armored knight on the right, knocking him backward. The two figures read as two clearly separate silhouettes even at native 64x32 (caped caster left, fire blast center, dark victim right) — it passes the silhouette test more cleanly than any other roll, and it is the most legible action composition in the spread. The red color identity is carried by the cape and the fire. Runner-up was gen 03 (the shaman summoning a full-bodied living-flame elemental), which is the most literally on-name "Flame Summoner" read with the purest red palette, but it lacks a visible victim taking the 2 damage, so it enacts the summoning without enacting the burn. Gen 5 wins on mechanic-enactment plus silhouette legibility.
