Nominee rationale:
Four large, distinct serpentine heads fan out across the frame, each with open
jaws and glaring red eyes, rising from a central coiled body over still swamp
water in a dim cavernous old-growth forest. This best satisfies the silhouette
test: a hydra's defining identity is "multiple necks branching from one body,"
and here that shape is large, unmistakable, and fully legible even at 64x32
(the model's strong default is a SINGLE-headed serpent -- gens 1, 6 fell to that
prior; explicit headcount fixed it). The mossy grey-green, lichen-weathered
palette reads as genuinely ancient/primordial, matching the card's heavily
colorless {5}{2}{G} cost and 7/7 body, and the dark full-scene atmosphere
harmonizes with the set's green-creature anchors (Forest Titan, Feral Stalker).
Clean green color identity; trample/force-of-nature menace conveyed through the
looming, jaws-open multi-head pose. The heads' large scale makes them readable
where busier rolls lose detail.

Close runners-up: gen 10 (five arching heads, dynamic posture, brighter swamp
scene) and gen 9 (five heads, sunlit, dynamic varied poses). gen 7 (clean row of
five heads) is solid but the necks read slightly repetitive/uniform.
