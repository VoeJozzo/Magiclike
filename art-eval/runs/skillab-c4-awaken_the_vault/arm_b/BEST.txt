Awaken the Vault animates a Land into a 3/3 creature until end of turn, so the art has to show the terrain itself standing up — not a creature posed on a hill. Gen 4 is the only roll that enacts that mechanic cleanly: a boulder-and-moss elemental leans up in a rising stance, made visibly of the same stuff as the ground (gray stone body, moss-green turf clumped over head and shoulders, a green glowing eye for the green color identity), and a fractured trench runs along the bottom of the frame showing a cross-section of grass over dark soil — you read it as the land having heaved itself up out of that gap. The silhouette is a strong, single readable hulking mass at 64x32, and the stone/turf/soil material contrast keeps it from reading as a generic golem. Gen 6 had a comparably good earth-beast subject but lost the load-bearing ground-cross-section (the "vault it rose from"), gen 5 added a distracting green orb in the sky and a more robotic body, and gens 1-2 read more as a creature standing on a hill than terrain in the act of rising. Gen 4 wins on mechanic-enactment plus color identity plus silhouette.
