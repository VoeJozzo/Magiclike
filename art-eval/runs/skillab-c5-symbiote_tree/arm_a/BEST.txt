This frame is the clearest enactment of Symbiote Tree's mechanic. On the left stands a recognizably humanoid treefolk (bark skin, mossy leafy head) with an arm reaching toward the right, where a wolf's entire back and shoulders are overgrown with the treefolk's green vines, glowing at the graft and sprouting bright leaves and pink flowers. That visible transfer of growth onto an ally creature reads as exactly what the card does on entering play: it grafts itself onto one of your creatures, swelling it larger (+1/+1) with new vine-growth climbing upward (granting reach). It is the only roll where all three beats land simultaneously — a true treefolk character (not just a tree trunk, which most other rolls collapsed into), a clear symbiotic bond, and a second creature visibly empowered. Two readable masses give a strong 64x32 silhouette, the scene is a full sunlit emerald forest (no sprite-on-void), and the green-plus-pink palette carries the card's green identity. Gen 7 was the runner-up (bigger, well-grafted wolf) but its treefolk lost the humanoid read; gen 1 had the bloom-transfer but a plain tree trunk. Gen 5 wins on whole-image gestalt.
