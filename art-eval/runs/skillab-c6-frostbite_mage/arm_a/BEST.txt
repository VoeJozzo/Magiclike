Frostbite Mage's mechanic is an ETB -2/+0 debuff: it arrives and SAPS an enemy creature's striking power without killing it. The art has to show the weakening happening to a victim, not just a wizard posing. Gen 6 enacts that most completely of any roll: the sapphire-robed frost mage (clean blue color identity, matching the {U} cost) is mid-cast on the left, a glittering ice-blue frost plume leaving his palm; the enemy soldier on the right is hunched and recoiling, his weapon arm crusted in chunky blue ice and his axe lowered/down — he reads as a fighter who just lost his strike, exactly the -2/+0. Two crisp, same-size figures, a snow-dusted courtyard background (a real scene, not a sprite on void), and the spell clearly travels caster -> victim. It beat the prettier gen 8 (whose curling frost tendril was gorgeous but whose victim looked braced rather than weakened) because the card is about the OUTCOME of the freeze, and gen 6 is the frame where the enemy visibly goes limp.
