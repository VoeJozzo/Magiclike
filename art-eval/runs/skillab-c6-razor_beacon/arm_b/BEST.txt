Razor Beacon is a colorless Artifact Creature Construct, 2/2 with flying, whose only mechanic is the flying keyword and whose name supplies two load-bearing nouns: "razor" (bladed) and "beacon" (a light source/signal). Gen 9 is the single frame that enacts all three facts cleanly. It is unmistakably airborne (worm's-eye view of the machine hovering overhead against a dawn sky with cloud) — satisfying the flying keyword, which the skill flags as the strongest literal-depiction convention there is. It is unmistakably a beacon: a white beacon-lamp on its underside casts a clean cone of light straight down toward the viewer, the most active, dynamic "beacon" beat in the whole spread. Its swept, spiked metal body delivers the "razor." It reads clearly as a colorless artifact construct — sleek dark brass with red/gold trim and a glowing amber core, no off-palette WUBRG color — with a strong centered silhouette that survives 64x32 and a real scene rather than a sprite on negative space. Gen 4 (the glowing blade-ringed lamp lighting a vault) is the runner-up for raw legibility of the beacon+razor idea, but its enclosed setting undercuts the flying keyword that this card is centrally about; Gen 9 keeps the construct clearly in the air.
