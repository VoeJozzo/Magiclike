This is the clearest enactment of Sicken's mechanic: the card gives an opponent's creature -2/-2, a wasting affliction that drains its strength, and this frame shows exactly that happening to the victim. A once-predatory wolf is rendered as an emaciated, mangy, near-skeletal husk — ribs and spine jutting through sagging sunken flesh, belly caved hollow, head hung low, hunched and trembling on splayed legs. The -2/-2 is not described, it is visible: a strong creature reduced to a dying one. It reads instantly at 64x32 thanks to a crisp lean-quadruped silhouette in side profile, and it holds black's color identity through the sickly olive-green plague palette and the dim rotted swamp behind it. Per the skill's subject-prior lesson, the wolf's naturally lean frame let the wasting land where brawnier subjects (barbarian gen 4, robust war-boar gen 2) fought the "sickened" intent and read as healthy. Among the wolf rolls it edges out gen 7's moodier twin because its brighter ground keeps the gaunt anatomy maximally legible — and legibility of the affliction is the whole point.
