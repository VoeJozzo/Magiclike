Best mechanic-in-art read of the spread. Healing Light's whole rule is gain_life on a player, so the art has to show life being restored to a wounded person, and this frame does exactly that: a white-robed cleric kneels over a downed knight in steel plate and presses both hands to his chest, where a brilliant white-gold burst erupts at the point of contact — the wound. The light is the brightest thing in the frame and it sits precisely where the healing acts, so the radiance literally enacts the life-gain rather than just decorating a holy theme (the trap gen 2's pretty-chapel-shaft fell into). Color identity is clean white-and-gold for a white card, both figures are fully visible and legible at 64x32 (kneeling-over-prone silhouette), and the night stone-courtyard background gives the glow something to read against. Gen 3 (angel of light) was the most striking silhouette and gen 7 (cradled orb) the most iconic, but both show healing as an entity/object rather than happening to a person; gen 8's seed-locked star was prettier but drifted off the wound. Gen 5 keeps the light on the victim, where the mechanic lives.
