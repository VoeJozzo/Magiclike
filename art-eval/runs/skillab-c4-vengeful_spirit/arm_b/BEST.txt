This roll is the only one in the set that enacts the card's mechanic rather than merely decorating its theme. Vengeful Spirit's defining rule is its death-vengeance: when this flying Spirit dies it destroys a target creature. The art shows exactly that act in progress — an unambiguous airborne ghost (hollow-eyed, bone-white, wispy trailing body, satisfying the Flying keyword's strong literal-depiction convention) swooping down and reaching its spectral claws into a victim who has already collapsed to the ground, withering and limp. The destruction is happening in-frame, with clear spirit-to-victim contact, which the other rolls lacked: across most generations the human subject-prior pulled the victim into an upright fighting stance (gens 1, 2, 4, 6), making the soldier read as the aggressor instead of the destroyed creature; the fix that cracked it was changing the victim to a collapsed, dying figure on the ground. Color identity is correct for a white card (cold pale-white spectral glow as the sole light source), the graveyard-at-night background gives real scene depth rather than a sprite on negative space, and the two figures stay distinct and legible at 64x32. Gen 7 pushed the victim too far into an illegible heap and lost the contact; gen 8 had a clean ghost but the spirit and victim were spatially separated so the kill was only implied. Gen 5 is the one frame where a strong spirit silhouette, a clearly-dying victim, and the moment of destruction all coexist.
