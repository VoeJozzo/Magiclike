This frame best enacts Roots and Branches' dual mechanic at 64x32. The card taps an enemy creature (roots binding it) and pumps your own (+1/+1, branches growing), so the art needs a readable two-figure duality. Gen 5 delivers the cleanest version of that: a soot-stained iron-armored enemy on the left versus a vivid leafy-green woodland warrior on the right, both fully visible head-to-knee at matched size with strong, distinct silhouettes and sharp color contrast (cold iron-blue against deep emerald) that immediately reads as "their creature vs mine." The pump/growth half is clearly enacted -- the right figure is visibly overgrown with sprouting foliage and bark, surging with vigor -- while the tap half reads as the enemy held in a tense, locked standoff. Across nine generations the model consistently refused to render roots literally binding the armored enemy (a strong subject-prior for "armored warrior standing," exactly the failure the skill predicts), so the binding is conveyed through the standoff posture rather than visible vines. Among all rolls, gen 5 has the best simultaneous color duality, silhouette legibility, and growth-enactment; gen 7 was a close second but its enemy figure was murkier and both figures floated more on the background.
