Best overall image and strongest mechanic-in-art in the set. Roots and Branches is a dual-target green spell: tap (bind) an enemy creature, pump (+1/+1, grow) a friendly one. The dual-creature framing carries the card's two-target structure (distinct opp/self creatures, both fully visible and the same size, facing off), and this frame nails it: a large gray wolf on the left vs. a boar on the right that erupts with a dramatic mound of bright leafy-green branches and new growth across its entire back -- the loudest, most legible "branches"/pump depiction across all nine rolls, and unambiguously green color-identity. Composition, sun-dappled old-growth lighting, and creature rendering are the cleanest of the run. Its one weakness is the understated "roots"/binding half on the wolf (green undergrowth hints at it rather than clearly ensnaring) -- a fixable, nameable flaw, the kind to forgive on a frame that is otherwise reaching for and landing a real scene, rather than down-ranking it in favor of a timid one. Gen 7 was the runner-up (both halves -- root-bound wolf + sprouting boar -- legible simultaneously) but is a rougher, darker, less striking image overall.
