This frame best enacts Threaten's mechanic rather than decorating its theme. Threaten seizes an opponent's creature, untaps it, gives it haste, and turns it on its owner for one turn. The image shows exactly that moment: a hulking armored beast-warrior wreathed in vivid crimson compulsion-fire, a glowing red core burning in his chest as the visible mark of imposed control, lunging with momentum (the haste) while a smaller former-master figure staggers/crouches to the right, clearly the threatened party. Critically, the red coercion fire trails along the ground and links the two figures, so the magic reads as the seizing force connecting controller and stolen creature, not as ambient battlefield flame. It has the strongest 64x32 silhouette of the set (a large looming dark mass versus a small recoiling shape), dominant red color identity matching the card's R cost, and clean two-figure legibility with correct relative scale. Gen 3 and the seed-locked gen 5 were close runners-up with the same composition, but gen 6 makes the controller-to-creature link and the "turned on its master" beat the most unmistakable.
