This frame best enacts the card. Righteous Cavalry is a vanilla white Human Knight (4/3, 2W+2) — a literal-card whose only handles are "Knight," "Cavalry," and the white/righteous identity, so the art's job is a clean, aggressive mounted-knight read. Gen 1 delivers the strongest silhouette at 64x32: an unmistakable warhorse-and-rider charging east, with a clearly readable raised lance — the one element that kept coming out faint in other rolls. Crucially it carries the *righteous* theme through a streaming white banner bearing a gold sun-cross emblem, which is the single most specific, intentional detail in the whole spread (it actually rendered, where most rolls gave only a blank banner). The dawn battlefield with the rising sun behind grounds it as a scene rather than a sprite-on-void. Its one fixable flaw — armor reading silver-grey rather than bright white — is exactly the kind of nameable, fixable error the skill says to forgive in favor of a frame that's reaching; the white identity is still carried by the banner, gold accents, and dawn palette. Gen 6 was the close runner-up (cleanest galloping motion, whitest horse) but its lance is nearly invisible, and a cavalry charge without a legible lance loses the point.
