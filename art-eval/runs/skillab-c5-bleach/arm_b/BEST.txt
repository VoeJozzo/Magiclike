Bleach does two things to a creature: it sets its color to colorless (the "set_color C" sticker) and then exiles it. This is the only roll that enacts BOTH mechanics on one canvas. The beast is a pale, colorless white alabaster husk (the color-drain), and it is visibly disintegrating from the front and edges into a billowing cloud of white dust and scattered glowing motes that flick off into the dark (the exile/erasure into nothing), with a faint last trace of drained color clinging to the lower body. It is white-aligned (cold white glow as the only light source), reads as a scene in a dim hall rather than a sprite on void, and keeps a legible quadruped silhouette. The cleaner single-mechanic alternates (gen 5, gen 7) render only "a white wolf" with no dissolution, so they read as a colorless creature but never show it being removed; gen 6's slightly softer edges ARE the erasure doing its job, which is exactly the card's payoff, so I rank the reaching frame over the timid ones.
