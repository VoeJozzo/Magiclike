This frame best enacts the card. Roots and Branches does two opposite things to two distinct creatures at once: it taps an opponent's creature (roots binding it down) and pumps your own (branches, growth). Gen 3 delivers the branches/pump half unmistakably -- the right-hand elk sprouts prominent branching antlers and carries a vivid patch of green leafy growth swelling on its back, wreathed in the green nature-magic the color identity demands -- while the dark wolf facing it across the frame gives the contested-encounter duality the card's name implies (one creature grounded and dark, one flourishing and empowered). It has the strongest gestalt and silhouette of the run: two flanking creatures of opposite character, fully visible and same-size, in a misty old-growth forest with real depth, harmonizing tightly with verdant_charm and giant_growth in the existing set. The one half the model would never render across six attempts on multiple subjects (literal roots gripping/shackling a quadruped) is sacrificed deliberately in favor of the half it paints best; among all frames where the pump landed, gen 3 is the cleanest and most readable.
