Crusader's Charm is a white modal sorcery whose three modes (Smite / Embolden / Sanctuary) make it the white analog of Verdant Charm, so the art should enact the modality the way Verdant Charm does: distinct radiances in distinct colors channeled by one figure. Gen 5 lands that best. The crusader's identity is unmistakable — polished silver-white plate with a gold cross on the breastplate and a second gold cross crowning the helm, purple hood and cape — anchoring it firmly in white/holy and harmonizing with the set's white soldiers. The modality reads through two clearly different-colored magical orbs held in spread hands: a warm amber radiance on one side, a bright cerulean radiance on the other, so the eye sees a single caster wielding multiple distinct powers rather than one effect. The silhouette is clean, balanced, and crisp at 64x32, the cathedral background gives it a real scene (not a sprite on void), and the lighting reads as holy magic rather than the fire-orange the earlier rolls kept drifting toward. The one honest flaw: the spell has three modes and the frame resolves to two distinct radiances, not three — the model consistently collapsed the third into a flanking pair across every reroll. I judged gen 5's superior finish, color identity, and legibility worth more than a third radiance the model refused to render cleanly; among all six it is the sharpest, most on-color, most readable enactment of "one crusader, multiple charms."
